## Phone settings and kit

- 4K 30, vertical, everything. 4K 60 for one or two walking clips only (slow motion in the edit).
- Tap and hold on her face to lock focus and exposure before every clip. Wipe the lens at every stop.
- 1x for most things, 0.5x only for a room that will not fit, 2x for hands and details.
- Free storage before leaving: a 4K afternoon is 20 to 40 GB. Battery bank and cable. Do Not Disturb on.
- The lines to camera at arm's length, indoors or out of the wind. Everything else gets music in post.
- Something to prop the phone against for the wides (a mini tripod, a clamp, or a water bottle and a book). The propped wide is the shot the whole edit stands on, so this is the one bit of kit worth having.

## What the edit needs, so the shooting serves it

- One day-in-the-life reel, about 60 s: ten to fifteen clips of three to eight seconds, chronological,
  a title card on the first clip, then one small centred time label per clip (Kia's clip 2), or her
  voice note as a subtitle if she sends one (clip 1). The propped wides carry it; arrive, hands and
  walking are the joins.
- A founder series from the interview: one answer per Reel, 20 to 40 s, the question as the title.
  Question 1 is the intro clip and can open the day-in-the-life reel (`friday-interview.md`).
- One story per stop, 10 to 15 s, a quickfire line or a line to camera as the spine. Four to six of them.
- Standalone moments, 20 to 30 s, in Kia's single-moment format: the gym with the bands, the client meeting.
- Branding is applied by us (D-059): the master mark and 162 C, the signature write-on, and her Lucy
  Wayne mark in her designer's font once the Canva share is opened (Q-041). Handle off (D-018). Music in post.
- A couple of raw clips go to her the same evening so she can post while it is still Friday.

## Risks and trade-offs

- Consent: a commercial gym usually wants the manager's OK to film, and clients can say no. Both asked
  of Lucy before the day; if the answer is no, the stop still yields arrive, leave and a line to camera.
- Light and sound: gyms are dim and streets are windy. Windows for the gym, shelter for the lines.
- Her lines to camera must stay clear of health and performance claims (voice guidelines, never-do 1).
  If one slips in, it is cut in the edit, not corrected on the day.
- Trademark (D-001): founder-facing footage does not hinge on the logo, so it is safe to post either way.

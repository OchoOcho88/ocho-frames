## Clip 1: the Tuesday day in the life (Hugo's pick, 22 Sep 2026)

`https://www.tiktok.com/@kia.buckley/video/7683093765602495764`, posted 8 Sep, 1:07 long, 2.8K likes.
Caption: "Tuesday are busy but so grateful for this life! So much in the works & I can't wait for
you all to see what we have been working on". Sound: "sonido original", her own voice.

What it actually is, measured from the frames:

- **The spine is a VOICEOVER recorded after the day**, past tense, her telling the day ("Jake and I
  get a coffee before work", "I went straight into an event coordinator meeting with Cinzia",
  "before I know it it was 4:00 and we had a wedding stylist meeting", "it is genuinely a dream
  come true"). Nobody talks to camera.
- **The text is a subtitle of the voiceover**, small white centred type in the lower third, one
  phrase at a time. Not big overlays.
- **Most shots are a propped phone, static, wide, at table height**, in the corner of the office
  or the meeting room, and she ignores it: her at the laptop, two people across a desk, three at a
  table. Real light, dim office, no styling. Long holds cut down to three to eight seconds each.
- **A few moving shots between them**: walking the warehouse aisles (someone else holding the
  phone), the coffee run, lunch at the desk shot from her own hand.
- Roughly twelve to fifteen clips in 67 seconds, chronological, no effects beyond the cuts and the
  subtitle. The warmth is in the voice, not the edit.

Consequence for Friday: prop the phone and let it roll at every stop, and get Lucy's voice
afterwards (a voice note is enough). The lines to camera become optional.

## Clip 2: the time-stamped day (Hugo's pick, 22 Sep 2026)

`https://www.tiktok.com/@kia.buckley/video/7680789198865059093`, posted 2 Sep, 0:54, 4.2K likes.
Caption: "For all my busy Perth girls, best work hack is HPM. I LOVE making my day in the life's,
she's backkkkk". Contact sheet: `clip2-timestamped-day-sheet.jpg` (ten frames, 2 s to 54 s).

What it actually is, measured from the frames:

- **No voiceover. The structure is a TIME LABEL on every clip**, one line of small white centred
  type mid-frame: "Coffee stop at HPM - 7am", "Arrive to work - 7:45am", "Admin, finance & more -
  8:00am", "12:00pm Lunch at HPM", "1:00pm Designer Meeting", "Zoom Meeting", a 3pm meeting, and a
  title card on the first clip, "Day in my life as the owner of Kikiva", over the car dashboard.
- **Ten to twelve clips of four to six seconds**, chronological, music under it.
- **The same propped static wide does most of the work**: her at the desk with the laptop, lunch
  from her own hand, two people over fabric swatches, the Zoom screen, the meeting room from the
  corner. Two handheld joins: the coffee walk with the phone held by a third person, and arriving
  through the office door waving at the camera.
- Real office, real light, no styling, no effects. The polish is the consistent label and the cut.

Consequence for Friday: this is the lower-effort cut for Lucy. Her "afternoon roughly" (the one
thing the 22 Sep email asks for) IS the label track, and no voice note is needed. Clip 1's
voiceover version stays as the option if she sends one.

## Clip 3: the live moment, Instagram (Hugo's pick, 22 Sep 2026)

`https://www.instagram.com/p/DZ6p0wTzIjz/`, posted 22 June, 0:36, 7.8K likes (the biggest of the
three). Caption: "Truely so grateful, wow!!! This community is really something else". Contact
sheet: `clip3-instagram-sheet.jpg` (ten frames, 1 s to 36 s).

What it actually is, measured from the frames:

- **One continuous take, no cuts, live sound.** The phone is propped on the desk across from her
  and never moves. She reacts to a milestone as it happens, holds her own phone up to the lens to
  show the number, talks to a colleague off camera, then to the camera.
- **One line of small centred text over the whole clip**: "Watch my business get over 6,000 visitors
  on our website for the first time", later "Thank god the website didn't crash".
- The set is her office: framed campaign photos on the wall behind her, the brand in the
  background without being pointed at. Blazer, pink cup, real light.

Consequence for Friday: the propped wide is not just coverage, it is the shot that catches a
moment if one happens. Leave it rolling. If anything lands on the day (a supplier reply, a sample,
a client saying yes), let her react to camera and show the phone to the lens, one take. If her
studio has the brand on the wall, that is the background for the propped wide.

## Clip 4: the product story carousel, Instagram (Hugo's pick, 22 Sep 2026)

`https://www.instagram.com/p/DXyFV3WE6XP/`, posted 1 May, about seventeen slides, read from
Hugo's screenshots (Instagram serves it login-only). Caption: "Almost 1 year in the making. The
Faith x Kikiva Collection design process and behind the scenes samples. Blood, sweat and tears
went into this collection."

What it actually is, measured from the slides:

- **A product origin story told as dated chapters**, one sentence per slide in the same small
  centred white sans, sitting in the lower third or dead centre: "April 2023. Everything started in
  2023, walking through factories in China, seeing firsthand how my activewear was being made",
  "What most people see as production lines, we saw as opportunity", "August 2025. Somewhere
  between factory floors and fabric swatches, the idea for the Scoop Shorts was born", "Existing
  machines couldn't create what we envisioned, so we built our own machine", "Back home, the real
  work started. Sketches, concepts, and endless samples", "Every millimetre mattered", "Too subtle,
  it lost its impact. Too dramatic, it lost its wearability."
- **The pictures are phone photos of the process, not of the product**: her on the factory floor in
  a black suit with a pink bag, the knitting machines, the supplier's team, a hand cutting pink
  fabric, a measuring tape on a waistband, a hand on a stack of samples in five colours, a flatlay
  on the office carpet with scissors and tape. The finished shorts appear only as samples.
- The polish is the consistency: one type style, one voice, one story arc across every slide.

Hugo's note: the reference for IF Lucy ever visits her manufacturer in China. That trip is Hugo's
idea, not a plan (confirmed 22 Sep), so it is not a loop. But the FORMAT does not need the trip:
a Sportif version is "how the bands came to be" from what already exists (her LA supplier
meeting footage when it arrives, the samples, the colour choice, the label, the pouch), one
sentence per slide. Voice guardrail if it is ever built: Kia's "we built our own machine" is a
custom-manufacturing claim; Sportif's colours are a curated pick from the factory range, so the
story is about choosing, not inventing (voice guidelines, never-do 4).

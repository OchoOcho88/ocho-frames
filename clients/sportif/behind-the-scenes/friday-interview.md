## The questions, by stop

Two to four per stop, matched to where she is. About five minutes of asking per stop, twenty to
thirty minutes across the afternoon. Hugo picks on the day; she does not have to answer all of them.

```
STUDIO, where it started (origin, quiet room, the intro clip first)
 1. Who are you, and what do you do? One sentence, as if to someone who has never met you.
 2. What made you start Sportif? What did you see in the shops that bothered you?
 3. You were going to do activewear clothing first. Why start with the bands and the strap instead?
 4. You studied fashion design, then styling, then training. How did one lead to the next?

GYM, the product in her hands (bands in frame, quiet corner or after the session)
 5. Show me the bands. What did you want them to look and feel like?
 6. How did you choose the colours?
 7. What is the ankle strap for, in your words?
 8. Who do you picture using them?

WALKING, the place and her life (Cross Street, the water, coffee in hand)
 9. Why Double Bay? What does this place mean to you?
10. What does a normal week look like for you?
11. Where do your ideas come from? People, places, moments.

WORK, the part nobody films (the desk, the laptop)
12. What is the hardest part of building a brand, the part nobody sees?
13. What is one thing you have learned so far that you wish someone had told you?
14. You have a rule about always being early. What are your other rules?

CLIENT MEETING, community (after the clients leave, or with their yes)
15. What do your clients teach you about what women actually want from activewear?
16. What does community mean for Sportif?

CLOSE, vision (the last stop, or the car)
17. Where do you want Sportif to be in five years?
18. LA in January. What does the Fit Expo mean for you?
19. Finish this sentence: Sportif is for the woman who...
20. If someone is thinking about starting something of their own, what would you tell them?

QUICKFIRE, any stop, for Stories (one line each, fast)
 Your morning ritual. Your coffee order, and where. The one thing always in your gym bag.
 One word for Sportif. And her own line, to camera: "Too fashionable not to wear."
```

Inputs:

## How to shoot an answer on a phone

- She holds the bands loosely in her hands for every answer, at every stop. The product is in every
  founder clip without being pointed at, and her hands have something to do.
- Phone at her eye level, 1x, arm's length to a metre away, propped or held still. Hugo sits right
  beside the lens so her eye-line lands next to it. Kia's biggest clip is exactly this: close, plain,
  nothing else in frame.
- Quiet spot, soft light from a window, exposure locked on her face. The gym is loud: do 5 to 8 in a
  quiet corner or after the session.
- Ask one question at a time and ask her to put the question inside the answer ("I started Sportif
  because...") so every answer stands alone as a clip.
- Twenty to forty seconds an answer. Two takes at most. Do not correct her on the day.
- Nod, do not speak: no "mm-hm" on the audio. Roll three seconds before and after.
- Audio is the phone's own mic within a metre. A wired lapel mic is a cheap optional upgrade, not
  a hire.

## Guardrails (voice guidelines, checked before the edit, never on the day)

- Question 7 may come back as "it tones your glutes". Health and performance claims are cut in the
  edit (never-do 1). Ask it as "what is it for", and keep the answer that describes the use.
- Question 6 may come back as "custom colours". The colours are a curated pick from the factory
  range (never-do 4). If she says custom, ask again as "how did you choose", and the edit uses that.
- Do not ask for a launch date. The launch waits on the trademark (D-001), and a date on camera
  becomes a promise (the teaser's end card already had to be pulled for this, Q-038).
- Do not ask about fabrics or sustainability for the range. The fabric is not locked (never-do 3).
- Do not prompt her to name other brands. If she names Kikiva or Anine Bing herself, that is hers
  to say, and the cut avoids it: the differentiator is her, not a comparison.
- Her PR days, the LA agency, the brand's earlier name (Le Sport Collectif, from her BoldJourney
  interview): background for Hugo, not questions.

## What the edit gets out of it

- A founder series: one answer per Reel, twenty to forty seconds, the question as the on-screen
  title in the house type, her name and the mark. Kia's format with Sportif's restraint.
- The intro clip (question 1) opens the day-in-the-life reel, or stands alone as the first post.
- The quickfire lines are Stories.
- Motion assets (the write-on, the mark) are applied by us (D-059).

## Trade-offs

- Spreading the interview across the stops costs about five minutes at each one, which is time
  the propped wide is not rolling on her working. Worth it: the day-in-the-life needs ten clips of
  her working, not thirty, and the interview is the part with her voice in it.
- Sending her the questions ahead makes her answers rehearsed. Sending nothing makes them
  rambling. The middle is the email's line: think about them, do not write them.

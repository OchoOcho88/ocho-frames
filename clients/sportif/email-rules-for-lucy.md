# Emailing Lucy: the house rules and the subject line template (D-058)

Lucy answers one line per topic, never quotes, and replies late. A multi-topic email
comes back as a one-liner with no anchor, and the workspace then guesses what she meant
(S043: "the three concepts", three weeks late, inside a July Canva thread holding four
jobs). The side stretch email in the same batch had its own subject and one question,
and it was the cleanest and fastest loop of the three. That is the pattern.

## Subject line template

```
Sportif | <the job, as she would name it> | <the ask, five words or fewer>
```

- Job: the thing, in her words, not ours. "Ball photo", "Fit Expo poster", "Double Bay
  filming day", "booth announcement". Never "email 02", "Q-027" or a script name.
- Ask: what she has to do. "one question", "your February photos", "pick a date",
  "nothing to do, for your files".
- Examples:
  - `Sportif | Ball photo in your peach | one question`
  - `Sportif | Fit Expo poster | need your February photos`
  - `Sportif | Double Bay filming day | pick a date, 1 to 10 October`
  - `Sportif | Your signature, animated | nothing to do, have a look`

## Body template

```
Hi Lucy,

<Only if she has just sent something we asked for: one line of thanks.>

<The question, one sentence, with the attachment named in it. Answerable in one word, phrased naturally, no "yes or no?" tag.>

<One or two lines of context, at most. Why it matters to her, not how it was made.>

<One line on what happens once she answers.>

Hugo
```

Every block above is its own paragraph with a blank line either side (rule 7). Drafts are written
that way so Hugo can paste and send without re-spacing.

## The rules

1. **One topic per thread, one question per email.** Two questions means two emails.
   She answers the easiest one and the other dies.
2. **Every job starts a fresh subject** in the template above. Never reply into the
   Canva "A design has been shared with you!" thread or any thread older than the job.
   **But when she answers inside one of OUR job threads, deliver in that same thread**
   (reply, attachments on it). A fresh subject for the delivery splits one conversation
   into two chains, which is the confusion this rule exists to stop (Hugo, S046: her 23 Sep
   answer on the room-in-peach thread was answered on that thread, not a new one).
3. **Question first, attachment named inside the question, answerable in one word**,
   asked the way a person would ask it: "Could we meet at 12:30 instead?", "Which of the
   three do you like?", "Which date suits you?". Never tack "yes or no?" onto the end
   (Hugo, S047: people don't speak like that); a plain question is already a yes or no.
   Explanation goes after, short.
   **Exception: when she has just sent something we asked for, open with one line of thanks,
   then the question in its own paragraph** (Hugo, S048: "Thank you for the link, the digital
   edition had a much sharper copy of the cover." then "Which of the three attached options do
   you like best for your grid, A, B or C?"). One line of thanks, never a paragraph of warm-up.
4. **One-liners by text, files by email.** She lives on her phone: marks arrive as phone
   photos and she posts within hours. A one-question text gets an answer.
5. **Ask for a fortnightly 15-minute call.** She has Calendly links in her bio. One call
   clears more than ten emails.
6. **When she is vague, ask her to send the thing back**, not to choose between our
   names for it (Hugo's call, S043).
7. **Let the email breathe.** A blank line between every part: the greeting, the thanks, the
   question, the context, the ending line, the sign-off. The question always stands on its
   own line, never tucked into the thanks or the context. (Hugo, S048: he re-spaced the
   three-options email by hand before sending; drafts now arrive spaced.)

## What this does not fix

Her replies stay slow regardless (the seven-day no-reply was flagged in June). The
structure makes her short answers usable when they arrive; it does not speed her up.
